# Business documents

Identify the reader, purpose, and requested decision. Use the user's supplied
facts and existing company template where available. Draft a concise memo for
information, a decision brief for a choice, or a proposal for requested work;
do not force all documents into the same template.

Put the purpose and recommendation early. Support them with relevant evidence,
options, tradeoffs, costs, and next steps in proportion to the decision. Keep
confirmed facts, estimates, and assumptions distinguishable. Use placeholders
for missing budgets, dates, owners, or approvals instead of inventing them.

When revising, preserve the author's intended position, material qualifications,
and exact identifiers. Remove repetition and unclear ownership. Do not turn a
draft into an official company policy or imply executive approval.

Deliver text in chat or the requested supported file format. If creating a file,
verify that it exists and can be opened or read back. Plain text with a renamed
extension is not a Word or PDF document. Publishing or sharing requires the
user's instruction and an available tool; otherwise deliver a reviewable draft.
