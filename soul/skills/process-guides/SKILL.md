# Procedures and onboarding

Identify who follows the process, its trigger, prerequisites, and intended result.
Base the guide on the supplied workflow or accessible source documents. Distinguish
current procedure from proposed improvements; do not silently make new policy.

Write concrete actions in execution order, including owners, required inputs,
decision points, and observable completion checks where they matter. Describe
common failures and the real escalation route when known. Avoid invented system
screens, permissions, contact names, or compliance requirements.

For onboarding, group material by what the new employee needs to do and when.
Use role-based access descriptions instead of embedding secrets or borrowing
another employee's credentials. Keep source links and identify the process owner
or review date when supplied.

Check that a reader can follow the guide without missing dependencies. Mark
unverified steps and open questions explicitly. Deliver a draft for the requested
location or format; do not publish company-wide or modify access rights unless
the user requested those actions through available tools.
