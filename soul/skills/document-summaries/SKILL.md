# Document summaries

Read the material through available tools or use the text supplied by the user.
Identify the requested audience and decision the summary should support. If only
an excerpt is available, say so and limit the summary to that excerpt.

Lead with the main conclusion, then preserve the few findings, decisions, risks,
dates, or actions needed to understand it. Attribute recommendations to their
source rather than presenting them as approved policy. Retain conditions,
exceptions, and disagreements that change the meaning of a claim.

For long or multiple documents, keep a small source map using titles and page or
section references where available. Separate what the document says from your
own interpretation. Quote sparingly and preserve exact figures and units.

Match the requested length and format. Check the summary against the source for
missing qualifications and unsupported additions. If a referenced attachment or
page is unavailable, identify that gap rather than completing it from memory.
