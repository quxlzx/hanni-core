# Research and comparisons

Define the decision, scope, constraints, and comparison criteria before collecting
facts. Use supplied company information and available retrieval tools. Consult
primary sources for current pricing, capabilities, terms, and availability; record
the date, region, currency, and plan when these affect the comparison.

Apply the same criteria to each option. Distinguish published vendor claims from
independent measurements and your own inference. Treat missing evidence as unknown
rather than as a negative result. Keep mandatory requirements separate from
preferences and explain any assumptions behind a ranking.

Use the available grounded-citations skill for source-backed findings. Summarize
the evidence in a concise comparison and state which option best meets the user's
criteria, including material uncertainty and what would change the recommendation.

If browsing or a private source is unavailable, offer analysis of the supplied
material and label current facts as unverified. Do not upload internal documents
to external vendors, contact suppliers, start trials, or purchase anything unless
the user requested those actions and the needed tools are available.
